## Description

Sheet metal is cut or formed in a press using dies. It is an umbrella family covering blanking, piercing, bending, drawing, embossing, and related operations.

## Strengths and weaknesses

Provides extremely high production rates and low unit cost at volume. Presses and dies are expensive, design changes are costly, and tearing, springback, wrinkling, and tool wear constrain designs.

## When to use

Stamping is the default for sheet-metal parts at high volume, meaning tens of thousands per year and up. Progressive dies cost tens to hundreds of thousands of dollars, and they pay back through cents-per-part pricing at dozens of strokes per minute. Lock the design early around springback, tearing, and die constraints, because changes after die tryout are expensive. Until annual volume reaches tens of thousands, laser cutting plus press-brake forming is almost always cheaper.

## Key numbers

Sheet thickness usually 0.2–6 mm · dimensional tolerance ±0.05–0.25 mm, down to ±0.025 mm with fine blanking · punch-to-die clearance 5–10% of stock thickness per side · progressive dies $20,000–300,000, 8–20 weeks lead time, 1–10 million hits of die life · press rate 20–100 strokes per minute, into the hundreds on small high-speed work · economic lot size tens of thousands a year and up, at piece prices in cents.

## Variants

### Blanking

A punch cuts a desired shape from sheet; the removed piece becomes the product. Fast and repeatable but produces burrs and surrounding scrap.

### Piercing and punching

A punch removes holes or slots from sheet. Highly productive but creates burrs and requires compatible tool access.

### Fine blanking

Sheet is sheared under strong compressive constraint using specialized tooling, producing smooth, nearly full-thickness cut edges. It works best within a moderate thickness band, so don't think of it as a "thick-sheet" process.

### Bending

Sheet is plastically bent along a line using a punch and die. Springback, bend radius, grain direction, and tool interference govern feasibility.

### Deep drawing

A flat blank is drawn into a die to create a cup, shell, or box. Draw depth, ductility, wrinkling, tearing, and corner radius are the main constraints.

### Embossing and coining

Material is compressed into fine die details or surface patterns. Creates texture, stiffness, or precise local features but imposes high die loads.

## Examples

Automotive panels, brackets, appliances, electrical contacts, washers.
