Head-to-head: which source

Source choice sets the cost, the safety case, and usually the wavelength of everything downstream. The rule of thumb: use the cheapest source whose beam quality and linewidth are good enough, and be honest about whether you need a beam at all.

SourceWavelengthsBeam qualityCostPick it when
LEDUV to NIRNone; wide emissionCentsYou need illumination rather than a beam. Half the lasers specified into systems could be this.
Diode laser375 nm to 3 umAsymmetric, correctable$1–500The wavelength exists and beam quality is adequate. Also the pump for everything below.
VCSEL850–940 nm mainlyRound, low divergenceCents at volumeConsumer sensing and short-reach links, especially in arrays. Wafer-level test is the cost advantage.
Fiber laser1.0, 1.55, 2 umNear diffraction-limited$10k–500kHigh average power with a good beam and low maintenance. The industrial default for metals.
DPSS laser1,064 nm and harmonicsGood, thermally variable$10k–100kHigh pulse energy or visible and UV output. Where fiber nonlinearity becomes the limit.
Gas laser193 nm, 633 nm, 10.6 umGood$5k–100k+The wavelength itself is the requirement: excimer for lithography, CO2 for organics.
Quantum cascade laser3–12 umModerate$5k–30kMid-infrared spectroscopy. Nothing else is coherent and compact in this band.
Ultrafast laserVisible to SWIRExcellent$50k–500kThermal damage is unacceptable, or the process is inherently nonlinear.

Which detector

Work out roughly how many photons reach the detector per measurement, then read down this table. Choosing a detector class with more gain than the signal requires costs money and adds noise.

DetectorSignal levelBandSpeedPick it when
PhotodiodeThousands of photons up200–2,600 nm by materialTo tens of GHzThere is enough light. The default, and everything else exists because of its limits.

PhotomultiplierSingle photonsUV to 900 nmNanosecondsLarge area matters in the visible or UV. Dead beyond 900 nm.
Image sensorModerate, per pixel200–1,100 nm siliconFrame rateYou need a picture. InGaAs arrays extend to SWIR at roughly 1,000x the cost per pixel.

How to modulate or move a beam

Two different questions get conflated here: changing the light in time, and pointing it somewhere. Speed and optical power are the axes that decide both.

DeviceSpeedPower handlingSide effectsPick it when
Acousto-optic modulatorTens to hundreds of nsHighDeflects and frequency-shiftsPulse picking and shuttering in a powerful beam, or you want the frequency shift.
Electro-optic modulatorTens of GHz and beyondModestNeeds a bias control loopAnything above about 10 MHz. All telecom transmitters live here.
Spatial light modulator60–500 HzLowZero order must be blockedThe wavefront itself is the thing you want to control: tweezers, adaptive optics, multi-spot.
Galvo scannerFew kHzHigh, large apertureMechanical wearMarking, cutting, confocal. The industrial workhorse for moving one spot.
MEMS mirrorTens of kHz resonantLow, small apertureAperture caps rangeSize and cost dominate: automotive LiDAR, pico-projectors.
