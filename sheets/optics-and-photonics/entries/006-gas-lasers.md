## Description
A gas laser excites a gas mixture with an electrical discharge and takes light from transitions in the gas itself. Three types still matter commercially. CO2 lasers emit at 10.6 um in the long-wave infrared and dominate cutting and engraving of non-metals. Excimer lasers use a noble-gas halide to emit deep ultraviolet at 193 or 248 nm, and are the light source for semiconductor lithography. Helium-neon lasers at 633 nm survive as cheap, extremely stable alignment and metrology references.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Each type persists because it does something no solid-state laser does as well. CO2's 10.6 um wavelength is absorbed strongly by organics, so it cuts acrylic, wood, and fabric cleanly where a 1 um fiber laser passes straight through. Excimer's deep ultraviolet photon energy breaks bonds directly, which is what makes it right for lithography and for corneal surgery. HeNe has exceptional frequency stability for the money. The weaknesses are shared: gas needs replacing, discharge electrodes and optics erode, wall-plug efficiency is 5–20%, and the systems are physically large.

## When to use
Use CO2 for non-metals and for thick-section cutting where its absorption suits the material, and accept the maintenance. Use excimer where the application needs deep ultraviolet photon energy rather than merely short wavelength, which in practice means lithography, ophthalmic surgery, and annealing. Use HeNe as an alignment and interferometry reference where a stable visible line matters more than power. Everywhere else, a diode, fiber, or DPSS laser is smaller, more efficient, and needs less service.

## Key numbers
CO2 emits at 10.6 um with wall-plug efficiency around 10–20% and powers to several kilowatts · excimer at 193 nm (ArF) and 248 nm (KrF), the deep ultraviolet used in lithography · HeNe at 633 nm, milliwatts, with excellent frequency stability · consumable gas and optics are ongoing costs · systems are bench or cabinet scale rather than modules.

## Examples
CO2 laser cutters across sign making and packaging; ArF excimer sources inside every deep ultraviolet lithography scanner; LASIK systems using 193 nm excimer; HeNe references in dimensional metrology and in classic interferometers.
