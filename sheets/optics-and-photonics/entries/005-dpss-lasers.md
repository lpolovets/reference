## Description
A diode-pumped solid-state laser uses a crystal, usually neodymium-doped YAG or vanadate, pumped by diode bars instead of a flashlamp. The crystal lases at 1,064 nm, and nonlinear crystals frequency-double or triple that to 532 and 355 nm, which is how most green and ultraviolet industrial laser light is made. Geometry varies: rod, slab, and thin disk, with the disk arrangement spreading heat over a large face so it can reach high average power with good beam quality.

## Strengths and weaknesses
A solid crystal stores far more energy than a fiber core, so DPSS systems produce high pulse energies at low repetition rate, which is exactly where fiber lasers struggle. The output is easy to frequency-convert, giving access to visible and ultraviolet wavelengths. Efficiency of 20–30% is well above the 1–3% of the lamp-pumped lasers they replaced. The weaknesses are thermal and mechanical: heat in the crystal causes thermal lensing that changes with power, so beam quality varies with duty cycle, and free-space optics need alignment and a clean environment.

## When to use
Choose DPSS when you need high pulse energy, ultraviolet or visible output, or a wavelength that fiber cannot reach. Marking and micromachining at 355 nm, laser rangefinding, and scientific pump sources are the standard cases. Prefer a fiber laser for continuous industrial cutting, where it is cheaper and needs less maintenance. When comparing quotes, ask about thermal lensing across the operating range rather than at one setpoint, because a beam specified at full power can be considerably worse at half.

## Key numbers
Typically 1,064 nm from Nd:YAG or Nd:YVO4, doubled to 532 nm and tripled to 355 nm · efficiency 20–30% against 1–3% for lamp-pumped · pulse energies from millijoules to joules, far above fiber at low repetition rate · thin-disk geometry reaches multi-kilowatt average power with good beam quality · unit cost commonly $10,000–100,000.

## Examples
355 nm DPSS lasers marking and drilling electronics; Nd:YAG rangefinders in military systems; TRUMPF thin-disk lasers in automotive welding; the green laser pointer, which is a tiny DPSS laser with a frequency-doubling crystal.
