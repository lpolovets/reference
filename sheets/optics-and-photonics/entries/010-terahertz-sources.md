## Description
The terahertz band sits between microwaves and the infrared, roughly 0.1–10 THz, and is awkward because it falls between the two technologies that work well on either side. Electronic oscillators run out of speed below it and semiconductor lasers run out of photon energy above it, which is why it was called the terahertz gap. Three approaches now fill it: photoconductive antennas gated by femtosecond pulses, optical rectification and difference-frequency mixing in nonlinear crystals, and quantum cascade lasers at the upper end, usually cooled.

## Strengths and weaknesses
Terahertz radiation passes through paper, plastic, ceramics, and clothing while reflecting from metal and being absorbed strongly by water, which is a useful combination for inspection. Photon energy is far too low to ionize, so it is safe for people. Many molecular and lattice vibrations fall in this band, giving spectroscopic contrast that infrared misses. The problems are power and cost. Sources deliver microwatts to milliwatts, atmospheric water vapor absorbs strongly over any distance, and a time-domain spectroscopy system costs $100,000 or more.

## When to use
Use terahertz for non-destructive inspection of non-conducting materials: paint thickness on cars, coatings on pharmaceutical tablets, voids in composites and foam. It is also established for security screening at short range. Do not expect it to work through walls or over long distances outdoors, because water vapor absorbs it heavily, and do not use it where an X-ray or ultrasound method already answers the question more cheaply. Treat any claim of long-range terahertz imaging with the atmospheric absorption spectrum in hand.

## Key numbers
Band roughly 0.1–10 THz, corresponding to 3 mm to 30 um wavelength · typical source power microwatts to milliwatts · non-ionizing, so no radiation safety case is needed · atmospheric water vapor absorbs strongly, limiting outdoor range to meters · time-domain spectroscopy systems commonly $100,000+.

## Examples
Terahertz paint thickness gauges used on automotive lines; pharmaceutical tablet coating inspection; airport body scanners operating at millimeter and submillimeter wavelengths; TeraView and Toptica as instrument suppliers.
