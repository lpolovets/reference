Glossary

Terms that show up in the device explorer and are not obvious from outside the field. Numbers are typical values, not specifications.

TermWhat it means
AberrationAny way a real optic fails to bring light to a perfect focus. Spherical aberration comes from surface shape, chromatic from index varying with wavelength, and coma and astigmatism from off-axis geometry. Multi-element designs exist almost entirely to cancel these against each other.
AchromatA lens, usually two cemented elements of different glasses, that brings two wavelengths to the same focus. An apochromat does it for three. Anything used with more than one wavelength needs at least an achromat, and a singlet will show colored fringes.
Beam qualityHow closely a beam approaches the tightest focus physics allows, usually quoted as M-squared or as beam parameter product. It is fixed at the source: no downstream optic can improve it, which is why source choice determines what spot size is achievable.
BlazeThe groove profile of a diffraction grating, angled so that most of the diffracted energy goes into one chosen order at one design wavelength. Without it a grating spreads light uselessly across many orders; with it, efficiency reaches 60–90%.
Chirped pulse amplificationStretching an ultrashort pulse in time before amplifying it and recompressing it afterwards, so the amplifier never sees a peak intensity that would destroy it. It made high-energy ultrafast lasers possible and won the 2018 Nobel Prize in Physics.
CoherenceHow well a wave keeps a fixed phase relationship with itself over time or space. Temporal coherence sets how far apart two paths can be and still interfere, and short coherence is exactly what optical coherence tomography exploits to isolate a depth.
Dark currentCurrent a detector produces with no light on it, from thermally generated carriers. It roughly doubles every 10 °C, which is why sensitive detectors are cooled and why InGaAs devices are far more temperature-sensitive than silicon ones.
DichroicAn interference filter used at an angle so it reflects one wavelength band and transmits another, splitting a beam by color rather than by intensity. It is how every fluorescence microscope separates the excitation light from the emitted signal.
DispersionRefractive index varying with wavelength, so different colors travel at different speeds. It causes chromatic aberration in lenses, spreads pulses in fiber, and stretches ultrashort pulses passing through any material, which is why reflective optics are preferred for them.
Excess noiseThe extra randomness an avalanche photodiode adds because its internal multiplication is a statistical process. It grows with gain, which means there is an optimum gain for any system rather than a best-possible one.
Extinction ratioHow much light gets through when a modulator or shutter is supposed to be off, expressed as the on-to-off ratio. It is the specification that decides whether a pulse picker actually isolates one pulse or leaks a train of small ones.
Faraday rotatorA material in a magnetic field that rotates polarization in a direction set by the field rather than by the light's direction of travel. Because the rotation does not undo itself on the way back, it is what makes a one-way optical isolator possible.
Interference coatingA stack of dielectric layers each a fraction of a wavelength thick, whose reflections add or cancel by design. The same technique makes antireflection coatings, 99.99% mirrors, and steep-edged bandpass filters, and all of them shift with angle of incidence.
Mode lockingForcing a laser's many longitudinal modes to oscillate with a fixed phase relationship so they interfere into a single short pulse circulating in the cavity. It is how femtosecond and picosecond pulses are produced.
Numerical apertureHow wide a cone of light an optic collects or emits, which sets both resolving power and depth of field. A microscope objective's resolution is proportional to it, and so is the difficulty of designing the objective.
Pockels effectRefractive index changing linearly with applied electric field in certain crystals, notably lithium niobate. It is the mechanism behind electro-optic modulators and behind the Q-switches that produce nanosecond pulses.
Quantum efficiencyThe fraction of incident photons that produce a countable carrier in a detector. Good silicon devices reach 80–95% in the visible, and it falls off sharply at the edges of a material's response band.
ResponsivityDetector output current per watt of incident light, in amps per watt. It is quantum efficiency expressed in the units a circuit designer needs, and it varies across the detector's response band.
Single-mode and multimodeWhether a waveguide supports one spatial field pattern or many. Single-mode fiber has a core around 9 um and preserves beam quality over long distances; multimode has a 50 or 62.5 um core, is much easier to couple into, and smears pulses through modal dispersion.
SpeckleThe grainy pattern produced when coherent light scatters from a rough surface and the scattered waves interfere. It is noise in laser illumination and imaging, and it is signal in interferometric measurement, depending on what you are trying to do.
Wall-plug efficiencyOptical power out divided by electrical power in. Diode lasers reach 50–70%, fiber lasers 30–40%, gas lasers 5–20%. Everything not converted becomes heat that the thermal design has to remove.
WaveplateA birefringent plate that delays one polarization axis relative to the other, converting linear polarization to circular or rotating it. It is specified at one wavelength and drifts with temperature, so it does not transfer between bands.
