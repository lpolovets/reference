# Reference Sheets

Markdown-driven reference dashboards (energy, robotics and manufacturing, defense and aerospace) rendered as searchable, filterable static sites from a shared engine.

- **Live site**: https://humbaventures.com/reference/ — GitHub Pages, redeploys automatically on every push to `main` (repo is `lpolovets/reference`; Pages mounts at `/reference/`). Each sheet builds into its own subdirectory (`/reference/manufacturing-processes/`, `/reference/battery-chemistries/`); the root is a landing page indexing all sheets.
- **claude.ai artifact: retired (2026-08-13).** A copy of the manufacturing sheet was published there and had to be republished by hand, so it fell several waves behind the live site — it never got glossary marks, key numbers, or the newer h2h tables, and a reader who found it got a worse sheet with no sign it was stale. Leo's call was to retire the channel rather than keep two copies in sync. **Do not republish it**, and do not point anyone at the old artifact URL. The `--artifact <slug>` build flavor is deliberately left in place: it costs nothing when unused, and it is the only thing that keeps the ASCII-only and no-external-requests constraints exercised, which is useful if the channel is ever revived.

## Writing style (important)

All prose (entry sections, guide.html, h2h.html, blurbs, ledes) must follow **STYLE.md** — Leo's plain, engineer-explaining-to-a-friend voice, not default AI style. Read STYLE.md before writing or editing any prose. Quick version: plain declarative sentences, concrete numbers, if/then advice, honest hedging ("usually", "roughly"); no aphoristic em-dash flourishes, dramatic vocabulary, or "not X — but Y" constructions.

**US spellings, always** — aluminum, fiber, meter, liter, center, license, program, gray, mold, tire, defense, vapor, modeling, traveling, canceled. The `british-spelling` rule in style.js enforces it. Three things are exempt and must never be "corrected": proper nouns carrying the spelling in their own name (Emirates Global Aluminium, UK Ministry of Defence, Guiana Space Centre, Fibre Glast, European Aluminium), anything inside a URL (`defence.gov.au`), and citation titles under `## Further reading` / `## Videos`. The rule is deliberately case-sensitive and lowercase-only for that reason. If you ever sweep spellings again, mask the citation sections by walking the `^## ` headings — a lookahead of `(?=^## |\Z)` silently fails to protect the *last* section of a file, because `\Z` is not a JavaScript escape and matches a literal Z.

**Technologies are not economic agents.** A battery does not earn, a plant does not want, a stack does not suffer. The owner, the operator, or the customer is who earns; the machine is what it is built to do. Leo's rule, in his words: "it's the owner/startup/etc that earns something... I'd say something more like *value is created by cutting the customer's bill*." Two rewrite shapes cover almost every case:

- **Justification sense** — "cooled sensors earn their cost past 3–5 km" becomes "cooled sensors **are worth it** past 3–5 km" (or "make economic sense past"). Also: "earns its place" → "is used because", "earns its premium" → "justifies its premium", "earns its keep" → "is still worth operating".
- **Return sense** — put the money on the owner or make it passive. "The plant earns by existing" becomes "**the revenue comes from** being available"; "a satellite over the Pacific earns nothing" becomes "**serves no customers**"; "the asset earns nothing" becomes "**with no revenue** during any of it".

The same applies outside money: `wants` → **needs** or **suits** ("LAES wants 8–12+ hour duty" → "LAES suits 8–12+ hour duty"), `is unforgiving` → **has to be exact**, `suffers` → **has** or **sees**. A sweep on 2026-08-10 fixed 84 of these. What is *not* a violation, and must not be "corrected": a **person or organization** as the subject ("mineral-sands miners earn a commodity margin", "a nation that wants a proven design", "project finance wants 10+ years"), causal `decides` ("format decides how the pack is cooled" means *determines*), and `tolerates` in its engineering sense ("tolerates abuse", "tolerates partial states of charge"). Two survivors are deliberate: manufacturing's "utilization needed to **earn** an acceptable return" (the business earns) and battery's "Starting an engine **wants** CCA; deep discharge **wants** amp-hours", which is punchy in a way that reads like Leo. This is not linted — the person/thing distinction needs a human — so it is a review habit, not a rule.

**Glossary definitions state a fact; they do not announce that the term matters.** The tell for AI-written prose here is a closing sentence that asserts importance instead of adding information: "The retail tariff **sets the economics**", "Whether swarf is recovered in-house **moves unit economics noticeably**", "The choice sets the whole manufacturing model, **not only the cost**". Compare energy-storage's *Firming*, which closes with numbers and a consequence, against the old *Behind the meter*, which closed with an abstraction — both used "rather than", only one reads badly. An audit of all 876 definitions on 2026-08-10 found the reliable offenders were few: 2 abstract-importance claims, 1 colloquial hand-wave ("because the grid **can't take it**"), and a handful of crammed rule-of-three parentheticals. **What is not a tic**: "rather than"/"instead of" (201 uses, nearly all carrying real information — "pays a plant for being available at peak rather than for the energy it delivers" *is* the definition), "which is why" (90 uses, usually earned by a preceding number), and vague magnitudes like "far less power" or "several times richer than ore", which are ordinary English. Those were flagged by a first-pass detector and deliberately left; do not sweep them.

**`node build/style.js` checks prose against STYLE.md's "Don't" list** and runs in CI on every push (`.github/workflows/style.yml`). It covers entry sections, guide/h2h text and the `sheet.json` strings, and skips `## Further reading` and `## Videos`, whose titles are the publisher's words. Every rule is high precision and the corpus currently scores zero — that was the design constraint, because a linter that fires on prose which is fine gets muted and then protects nothing. Rules tried and dropped for that reason are recorded at the top of the file: em dashes per paragraph (203 paragraphs use a legitimate matched pair, and the worst "offenders" were table rows that only look like paragraphs once tags are stripped), bare "not just X" (16 uses, all ordinary contrast), and the words "seismic" and "unlock" (real technical vocabulary here). The aphorism rules require a negation or a copula after the dash, which is what separates `the constraint is not geology at all — it is which refineries bother` from `There is no remote attack on an inertial unit — it senses only its own motion`. **If a rule ever fires on prose that reads fine, fix the rule, not the sentence.**

## Workflow rule (important)

**Review-then-push.** Make changes locally, demonstrate them (screenshot or local preview), and only commit/push/republish after Leo explicitly approves. A request to change something is not approval to publish it. One approval covers one push.

## Layout

- `sheets/<slug>/` — one directory per reference sheet:
  - `sheet.json` — all sheet config: titles, lede, unit words, group label, the seven parts/classes (each part takes an optional `blurb` — a short lowercase purpose phrase, under ~55 chars, shown on its explorer tile; avoid raw `&`, it's injected unescaped), and **facet definitions** (`id`, frontmatter `key`, `label`, `type` multi|range|single, `color` var, `options`, `order`, optional `chipLabels`/`tagPrefix`/`tip`/`required`/`tagRow`), plus `extraSections` mapping optional markdown sections to card labels.
  - `entries/NNN-slug.md` — one file per entry. Frontmatter: `number`, `name`, `part`, `group`, plus one key per facet (keys validated against sheet.json). Sections: `## Description`, `## Strengths and weaknesses`, `## When to use` (prescriptive: when to pick it, when to avoid it, and the usual alternative; present on all entries since 2026-07-25), then optional `## Variants` (with `###` entries), `## Videos`, and the sheet's remaining extra sections (`## Examples`, `## Economic profile`).
  - `guide.html` — the sheet's guide-tab content (raw HTML fragment using shared classes: `.tblwrap`, `.bands`, `.callout`, `.qlist`).
  - `glossary.html` — the Glossary tab: a `.tblwrap` table of alphabetical terms drawn from the sheet's own entries, each defined in 1–3 plain sentences. It used to be the last section of `guide.html` and moved out on 2026-08-10 so it could be its own tab. Optional in the same way as `h2h.html`: no file, no tab. The bar reads **explorer, glossary, guide, h2h** — look something up before reading advice about it — and that order lives in two places that must agree, the `{{GLOSSARY_TAB_BTN}}` slot in page.html and the `tabs` array in app.js. **Scope: any term that appears in the explorer and that a layperson would not know**, not just the headline concepts; spinneret and mandrel belong in it. A useful way to find candidates is to score words by how concentrated they are in one sheet, since general English shows up in all 18 and jargon does not, but that is a candidate list rather than an answer — raw frequency also surfaces "economical" and "pieces".
  - **Glossary terms marked in entry prose.** A glossary row may declare the word forms it should be recognised by: `<tr data-match="sinter|sinters|sintered|sintering">`. Those words then get a faint dotted underline in the explorer and show the definition on hover, focus, or tap. **Opt-in by design** — a row without `data-match` is never marked, and a sheet with no marked rows ships byte-for-byte what it shipped before, which is what let this be added one sheet at a time. All 18 sheets now carry patterns: **871 of 876 rows**, 3,582 marks, 2–9 per card and never more than 1.7% of a card's words. The five deliberate holdouts are manufacturing's "Green" (green sand, green hydrogen, the color) and "Tolerance" (Leo's call — kept in the glossary, too common in the prose to underline), hydrogen's `Nm³` and space's `TT&C` (neither can take a `\b` boundary, since `³` and `&` are not word characters), and nuclear's `Q (fusion gain)` (a bare single letter would match every stray Q).
    - **Forms must be literal words, and the build enforces it.** `checkGlossaryTerms` in build.js drives each declared form through the real regex *and* the real lookup, and fails the build naming the sheet, term, and form. This exists because the failure it catches is silent: a form like `carburiz\w+` matches the alternation, then fails the literal `TERM_BY_WORD` lookup and leaves the word plain. Manufacturing shipped three of those for weeks, costing real marks on carburizing and nitriding, and nothing in the output showed it.
    - **Longest form first.** Alternation takes the first branch that matches at a position, not the longest, so declared order marks "redox" inside "redox flow" and "prussian blue" inside "prussian blue analog" — both separate rows in the battery sheet. render.js and the validator both sort all forms by length descending. Note this only bites when the shorter form is a *prefix* at the same position: `state of charge` inside `partial state of charge` is fine, because the engine reaches the longer form's start position first.
    - **An all-caps form only marks an exact-case hit**, derived automatically from the declared casing rather than a per-row flag. Matching has to stay case-insensitive because prose capitalizes at sentence starts, but an acronym is a different word from the ordinary one sharing its letters. Nine real false positives came from exactly this: weather **fog** marked as fiber-optic gyro (5), sales "design-**ins**" marked as an inertial unit (3), and **Amazon Leo** marked as low Earth orbit (1). Mixed-case forms like `Ra`, `mAh` and `CubeSat` stay case-insensitive. When a form is skipped for wrong casing the term is *not* consumed, so a properly capitalized use later in the same card still marks. Watch for mixed-case acronyms whose lowercase form is a common word — `FiT` for feed-in tariff was dropped for this reason ("fit").
    - Marking happens in `fmt()` in render.js, on escaped text with markdown links still in their unconverted form. That ordering is load-bearing: a term inside a URL cannot be hit because the URL is not text yet, and link labels are skipped because they are citation titles. Doing it after link conversion would let "die" inside an `href` rewrite the link.
    - **One combined case-insensitive alternation, not one regex per term.** Measured on the 212-entry manufacturing sheet: 43 separate regexes cost 7.4 ms per re-render against 0.7 ms combined, and the client re-renders on every filter, sort, and search keystroke. Real cost of the whole feature is 1.5 ms → 2.6 ms per re-render, and +0.2% gzipped.
    - Each card gets its own `seen` set, so a term is marked the first time it appears in that entry and left plain afterwards. Definitions are not repeated per occurrence: the span carries only a key and `#gtip` is a single shared tooltip element, filled from `SHEET.terms`.
    - When choosing patterns, be comprehensive about inflections but leave out words that are ordinary English in another sense. Verify by rebuilding and reading the marked words in context — `die`, `flash`, `blank`, `draft`, and `bore` all looked risky and all turned out clean in this corpus, and so did `concentrate`, `duration`, `stack`, `overlay`, `cadence`, `pose` and `glass-filled` on the newer sheets, but that was checked rather than assumed. The check that pays is grepping the built pages for marked words that are short and lowercase, then for marked words whose casing does not match the declared acronym; that is what surfaced fog/ins/Leo. The one class of error that survived review until it was read in context is a **compound-versus-process** mismatch: `nitride` in "silicon nitride" is a ceramic, not case hardening, so Case depth matches only `nitriding`/`nitrided`.
  - `h2h.html` — the "Head-to-head" tab. Opens with one comparison table of the 5–8 high-level options that actually compete, then **2–3 further tables covering the sub-decisions within a class** (a single top-level table is too coarse on its own). Every table ends with a "Pick it when" column (last `<td>` renders muted; first `<td>` is bold/nowrap so keep row names short) and carries real numbers in the middle columns. Optional file: if absent the tab is omitted (tab label via `h2hTab` in sheet.json, default "Head-to-head"). Uses the same shared classes as guide.html — the view reuses `class="guide"` styling.
  - `images/NNN.jpg` (optional) — per-entry illustration keyed by zero-padded entry number (.jpg/.png/.webp); rendered at the top of the card body on the website flavor only (the artifact blocks external requests).
  - `images/part-<part id>.jpg` (optional) — per-**class** illustration shown on that class's explorer tile, website flavor only. Deliberately sparse: some classes have no subject that draws well, and one is meant to be left out rather than faked. The moment any class on a sheet has an image, the classes without one reserve the same block of space (`.pimgnone`), so every tile's title still starts on the same line — a single missing image otherwise pulls one title to the top of its tile and reads as a bug. A sheet with no class images at all emits neither the image nor the spacer, so it ships exactly the markup it shipped before.
  - `images/prompts.json` (optional) — the generation prompts for that sheet's illustrations, so the set is reproducible: `{"anchor": <entry number>, "style": "<optional override>", "prompts": {"1": "...", ...}, "partPrompts": {"1": "...", ...}}`. Consumed by `build/generate-images.js`; the build copies only image files out of `images/`, never this.
- `shared/` — everything common: `theme.css` (all styling, inlined into every page at build), `page.html` (skeleton with `{{...}}` placeholders), `app.js` (the generic explorer engine, driven by an injected `SHEET` config), `logo.html`.
- `build/build.js` — plain-Node build; auto-discovers `sheets/*/sheet.json`.
- `.github/workflows/pages.yml` — build + deploy on push.

## Build

```bash
node build/build.js                       # -> site/<slug>/ per sheet (page + data.json) + site/index.html landing
node build/build.js --artifact <slug>     # -> dist/<slug>.artifact.html (fragment for the claude.ai artifact)
node build/build.js --check               # validate every sheet, write nothing
node build/build.js --allow-incomplete    # build, skipping sheets that have no entries yet
```

No dependencies. The build validates frontmatter and video lines and fails naming the offending file. `--check` runs exactly those validations and writes nothing, which is what to use when several agents are editing entries in parallel (a full build would have them racing on `site/`). It isolates each sheet, printing `ok` / `pending` / `FAIL` per slug and exiting non-zero only if something actually failed, so one agent's broken file doesn't mask everyone else's status.

**A sheet still being written doesn't fail the run.** With no entry files at all it reports `pending`; with some of its groups still empty it reports `partial` and lists them. Both skip the `groupBlurbs` cross-check, which would otherwise fail against a half-populated group set and take every other sheet's status down with it. A real build refuses to ship either state, since the result would publish as an empty or holed page; pass `--allow-incomplete` to drop those sheets from the build and preview everything else. The landing page and the about page count only the sheets that actually built. Verify UI changes by serving `site/` locally (there's a `.claude/launch.json` "site" config on port 8741) and loading `/<slug>/` (add a throwaway `?v=` query when re-testing — the preview browser caches hard).

## Conventions and gotchas

- **Adding a sheet**: create `sheets/<slug>/{sheet.json,guide.html,entries/}`; the build and landing page pick it up automatically. Reuse `--p1..--p7` for part colors and `--fa..--fe` for facet colors (blue/gold/red/green/purple — all defined once in `shared/theme.css` for both themes; add a new var there if a sheet needs a sixth facet).
- **Facet types**: `multi` = array field, one tag per value; `range` = ordered array collapsed to a "Prefix: First–Last" tag (labels must be single words or the dash collapse reads badly); `single` = scalar, `none`/omitted allowed unless `required` (no tag rendered when absent, and entries without it match only when that facet filter is empty).
- **`tagRow: 2`** on a facet puts its card tags on their own second line under the others, right-aligned to match (battery-chemistries uses it for applications). Facets without it share the first row with the expand caret.
- **Shared style**: all styling lives in `shared/theme.css` and is inlined into every page at build — edit it once and every sheet (and the landing page) changes together. Keep the label column (`.facet .flabel`, 108px) wide enough for the longest facet label across all sheets; long labels wrap badly otherwise.
- **Further reading**: 1–2 links per entry in a `## Further reading` section (last extra section), one line, links `[Title (Publisher)](url)` separated by " · ". Policy: canonical sources only — institutional reports, trade-association primers, vendor *educational* content, open-access reviews; one practical + one deep link where both exist; **every URL must be fetch-verified live before inclusion**; skip the section rather than pad (deliberately empty as of 2026-08-09: manufacturing 025 slush casting only — the other three long-standing gaps were filled that day from NPTEL lecture PDFs, an EPA AP-42 section, American Machinist, IIBEC, and The Fabricator; slush casting still has no canonical source, only anonymous reference sites, a paywalled ASTM ingot spec, and a `vlabs.ac.in` page whose TLS certificate has expired). Known bot-blockers that still work in browsers: ASM Digital Library, Edmund Optics, COBOD, Panasonic PDFs, **The Fabricator** (Cloudflare interstitial to every fetcher; verify its articles by loading them in the browser pane), **`pubs.usgs.gov`** (403s WebFetch, 200s to a browser user agent — `curl` with a browser UA plus `pdftotext` works), **`gao.gov`** (403s with a 399-byte body even to a browser UA in curl, on both `/products/` pages and `/assets/*.pdf`, but WebFetch reads the product pages fine and the browser pane works too — a curl-only check wrongly rejects it, and GAO product numbers are exactly the kind of identifier that gets invented, so verify every one), **`nickelinstitute.org`**, **`intlmag.org`**, **`tms.org`**, and **`icao.int`** (its HTML pages verify via WebFetch even though curl is refused). Sites that block fetchers entirely (avoid): ScienceDirect, Wiley, MDPI direct (use PMC mirrors), ACS, RSC, NRC, IAEA PDFs, SemiEngineering, NDE-Ed.org, automate.org, Festo, **`semi.org`**, **`pubs.aip.org`** (AVS/AIP — use arXiv or PMC for the same papers), **IEEE Spectrum** (serves account prompts with partial content, so a fetch looks like it worked), `crsreports.congress.gov`, `sto.nato.int`, `dosits.org`, `analog.com`, `defence.gov.au`, and most `.mil` hosts (`af.mil`, `army.mil`, `navair.navy.mil`, `navsea.navy.mil`, `defense.gov`). Two other traps: **`inst.eecs.berkeley.edu`** now redirects its course PDFs to a CAS login, and **`scis.scichina.com` is HTTP-only** — port 443 times out, and WebFetch upgrades to HTTPS, so it can never be verified that way. **PMC** serves a CAPTCHA shell to fetchers; get titles from the NCBI E-utilities API instead. **`cdc.gov` 403s every fetcher** with a 469-byte "Access Denied" page, including the Pink Book chapters, so cite NIAID or WHO for vaccine background instead.
  - **The physics-journal publishers all closed in 2026, and arXiv is the way around them.** `iopscience.iop.org` answers 200 and then redirects to a Radware bot-manager CAPTCHA — it used to be the recommended substitute for AIP and no longer works. `journals.aps.org` returns a Cloudflare 403, and `scipost.org` serves "Making sure you're not a bot!". **`nature.com` is the dangerous one**: it answers **HTTP 200 with the title "Client Challenge"** and redirects to `idp.nature.com`, so a status-only check passes it while the body is a wall. Nearly every paper behind these hosts has an arXiv preprint at the same DOI, and `arxiv.org/abs/<id>` fetches cleanly and carries the real title, so cite the preprint and name the journal in the title text. Two more found the same day: `publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu` serves an HTML "Request Rejected" page for `.pdf` URLs, and **`nsa.gov` refuses curl and WebFetch with an Akamai 403 but reads fine through `cite.js check` and the browser pane** — worth knowing because NSA's CNSA and QKD guidance is directly on point for the quantum sheet.
  - **An arXiv id is exactly the kind of identifier that gets invented**, and the failure is quiet because every well-formed id resolves to a real paper. During the 2026-08-14 pass a citation for topological-qubit parity measurement was written against `arXiv:2502.12252`, which is a real paper titled *Roadmap to fault tolerant quantum computation using topological qubit arrays* — a different subject. It was caught only by reading the title back off the abs page. Always `cite.js check` the abs URL and copy the title it returns.
  - **Search APIs beat WebSearch for finding citable documents**, and matter because `WebSearch` is capped at 200 calls per session — six agents hit that ceiling during the 2026-08-14 pass and finished on APIs instead. All of these return real ids and real titles, so they are guess-proof in a way a search snippet is not: **OSTI** `osti.gov/api/v1/records?q=` (DOE and national labs), **USGS Publications Warehouse** `pubs.usgs.gov/pubs-services/publication/?q=…&page_size=N` (far better than searching, since `pubs.usgs.gov` blocks WebFetch), **DOAJ** `doaj.org/api/search/articles/…` for open access, the **arXiv API** (HTTPS only — plain HTTP to `export.arxiv.org` returns an empty body), and NCBI E-utilities via `cite.js search`. `html.duckduckgo.com` starts serving CAPTCHAs after a few queries and is not a reliable fallback.
  - **More hosts that refuse automated clients** (found 2026-08-14, all verified as walls rather than rot): `opencompute.org` (403 even to a browser UA, which is painful because OCP is the natural source for rack and power-shelf material), `optica.org`/`opg.optica.org` and `photonics.com`, `thorlabs.com` (silently redirects deep tutorial URLs to its main navigation, so a "working" link lands nowhere useful), `ti.com/lit` PDFs (404 as HTML), `qsfp-dd.com` (no response at all), `publications.jrc.ec.europa.eu` and `e3p.jrc.ec.europa.eu` (244-byte "Request Rejected", including for direct `.pdf` paths), `research.aalto.fi/files/*` (403, though its record pages serve fine), `datacenters.lbl.gov/resources/*` (403 with a misleading title, while `/sites/default/files/*.pdf` is fine), `congress.gov`, `internetsociety.org`, `yukon.ca`, `gardguide.com`, `tomra.com`, `link.springer.com`, `escholarship.org` (HTTP 202 with a zero-byte body), `acl.digimat.in`, `home.engineering.iastate.edu`, `pes-psrc.org/kb/`, and `nerc.com/pa/RAPA/*.aspx` (though `nerc.com/programs/*` serves fine).
  - **When a fetch fails, vary the user agent in both directions before concluding the document is gone.** Four hosts this run refuse a *browser* user agent and serve a plain one: `onsemi.com` PDFs, `bls.gov`, ESMATS PDFs, and `fcc.gov` (which additionally needs Node's own `fetch` rather than curl). The reflex this file used to recommend — retry with a browser UA — produced false negatives on all four, and agents dropped good citations because of it.
  - **An empty body is not a verified document, and `cite.js` used to say it was.** `eur-lex.europa.eu` answers HTTP 202 with zero bytes to every client, and the checker printed `OK`, which is the one word an agent reads before pasting a citation. Fixed 2026-08-14: an empty body now grades `BAD`, and sub-1KB HTML with no title is flagged as a probable wall. EU legal texts remain uncitable at any URL — name the regulation and article in prose instead.
  - **A dead project's domain can be repurposed.** `unmanned-ship.org`, the old MUNIN autonomous-shipping site, is now a Vietnamese sports-streaming page answering HTTP 200. A link checker sees success and says nothing, so anything cited from memory against a defunct project needs its host re-read, not just its status checked.
  - **Search routes when the usual ones run out.** WebSearch's 200-call cap is shared across every agent in a session and went in the first hour; OpenAlex has a separate daily budget that also ran out. What worked after that: **OpenAIRE** (`api.openaire.eu/search/publications?keywords=…&format=json`), **NASA's own WordPress search** (`nasa.gov/wp-json/wp/v2/search?search=…`, the only reliable way to find live nasa.gov paths since the migration broke most of them), **UNCTAD's bulk statistics API** (`unctadstat-api.unctad.org/bulkdownload/…`, open even though every UNCTAD page is Cloudflare-walled), **ISARC** (`iaarc.org/publications/search.php?series=1&query=…`, the parameter is `query` not `q`), **openFDA** for guess-proof 510(k) and PMA numbers, **`sec.gov/Archives/`** for company economics, and **grepping a publisher's `sitemap.xml`** when its own search is JavaScript-only. `cosmicspace.org` is the best source for in-space servicing and depot material.
  - **`onsemi.com` PDFs are the inverse of every other wall in this file, and the standard fix makes it worse.** A *browser* user agent gets HTTP 403 with a 431-byte HTML body; plain `curl` with no UA override gets the real 3.3 MB PDF. An agent reported it as a lying server on the strength of the browser-UA test alone and dropped a perfectly good citation. `links.js` grades it OK, which is right. Verified on `tnd6278-d.pdf`, whose cover reads `TND6278/D Rev. 6, October 2021, Power Factor Correction - Optimization Options`. Before concluding any document is gone, try the fetch *without* a browser UA as well as with one.
  - **An ST application-note URL slug can name a different document than the PDF it serves.** `an4638-designing-a-highefficiency-forward-converter-with-the-l6565-quasiresonant-controller` returns a PDF whose cover is *Welding machines: V and HB series IGBTs on two-switch forward converters*. Same class as the ICH Q6B underscore case: a descriptive slug is not a title, and only `pdftotext` on the cover catches it.
  - **Search APIs, ranked by what they were actually good for** during the 2026-08-14 seven-sheet build, once `WebSearch` hit its 200-call session cap in the first hour. **OpenAlex** (`api.openalex.org/works?filter=title.search:…,is_oa:true`) was the most productive for applied engineering, especially filtered to university repository hosts — `unsworks.unsw.edu.au`, `idus.us.es`, `orbit.dtu.dk`, `vbn.aau.dk`, `ris.utwente.nl` all serve full text to a fetcher. **OSTI** remains the right tool for anything DOE, and its `records/<osti_id>` endpoint returns `report_number`, which is how you name the real publishing institution (`PNNL--38364`, `NREL/JA-5K00-81844`) instead of labeling the citation "OSTI". **arXiv is nearly empty for applied power electronics** — full-text queries for modular multilevel converter, multilevel converter and dual active bridge returned nothing usable, which is worth knowing before spending calls on it.

**Verifying a PDF needs `pdftotext`, not WebFetch.** WebFetch hands the model raw binary for a PDF and it will confabulate a plausible title from the URL. Download it and read the actual title page. Two examples of the sharpest failure mode, where a URL returns a real document that is **the wrong document**: `database.ich.org/sites/default/files/Q6B_Guideline.pdf` serves Good Clinical Practice instead of Q6B (the real one is `Q6B%20Guideline.pdf`, with a space not an underscore), and an `oecd-nea.org/jcms/pl_51110/unlocking-reductions-in-the-construction-costs-of-nuclear...` slug serves *Projected Costs of Generating Electricity, 2020 Edition*. A status code, a content-type, and a plausible file size all pass while the content is something else. Only reading the cover catches it. The clearest demonstration: a *guessed* USGS fact-sheet number, `pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2024/3033/fs20243033.pdf`, downloads a clean valid PDF — about invasive blue catfish in Chesapeake Bay, not the geologic-hydrogen report the guesser wanted. - **Key numbers**: every entry carries a `## Key numbers` section right after `## When to use` — one line, 4–7 quantities separated by ` · `, no prose sentences. It should restate the numbers already in the entry's other sections rather than introduce new claims, so it never contradicts them. Which quantities suit which sheet is a per-sheet decision (tolerance/finish/lot size/tooling cost for manufacturing; Wh/kg, cycle life, $/kWh for batteries; endurance/range/payload/unit cost for uncrewed systems).
- **Derived conclusions: show the chain or mark it.** Some of the sharpest claims in these sheets are arithmetic nobody publishes — the price at which refuelling beats a life-extension pod, the delivered $/MWh of hydrogen power, the cost per gram of antibody off a Protein A column. Those are legitimate and often the reason an entry is worth reading, but a reader must never mistake one for a looked-up figure. Two acceptable forms: **show every step** so the arithmetic is visible (hydrogen 031 does this — 33.3 kWh/kg, 20 kWh back at 60%, so 50 kg/MWh, so $200–300/MWh at $4–6/kg), or **state plainly that it is derived** when the conclusion is given without the steps (space-launch 031 says "derived here, not published"). What is not acceptable is a computed conclusion sitting in Key numbers looking exactly like the sourced quantities beside it.
- **Videos**: max 3 per entry, format `- <YouTube watch URL> — Title (Channel, N minutes, band+ views)`. Policy: embeddable, playable, not live, ~1.5–20 min (≥1 min for popular animations), prefer popular/maker-community explainers; leave the section out if nothing good exists. View bands: 1k/5k/10k/50k/100k/500k/1m/5m, or "under 1k views".
- **The video line is split into fields at build time** by `splitVideoMeta` in build.js, giving `{id, t, c, dur, vw}` rather than one string: runtime renders as a badge on the thumbnail, channel and views as a caption under the title. The markdown format is unchanged, so `yt.js audit` — which reads the entry files, not `data.json` — is unaffected. **Do not match the trailing parenthetical with a regex on `" ("`.** Titles and channel names both contain parentheses (`JoVE (Journal of Visualized Experiments)`, `Sumitomo (SHI) Demag`, `Tesla 4680 Teardown (Part 2)`), and the ambiguity cuts both ways: a greedy match splits the Sumitomo case wrong, a lazy one splits the Part 2 case wrong. Scan backwards from the end for the balanced group that closes the line, which is unambiguous — that parses all 2,061 lines, where the obvious regex leaves 21. A line with nothing to split off keeps its title and simply renders without a caption.

In the artifact flavor there are no thumbnails, so duration joins the caption and each video takes its own line — joined by `<br>` rather than a middot, because the captions are themselves middot-joined and running them together reads as one string.

- **Monthly link rot check**: `.github/workflows/linkcheck.yml` runs `build/links.js` (further reading) and `yt.js audit` (videos) on the 1st of each month, and files or updates one issue per month when either finds rot. Both are runnable by hand: `node build/links.js [sheet-slug]`.
  - **The design problem is false positives, not fetching.** Most of the good sources here refuse automated clients, so a naive checker reports dozens of dead links a month and gets ignored by the second month. Results are graded instead: **DEAD** (404, 410, host does not resolve) fails the run; **SUSPECT** (200 but the shape lies) and **BLOCKED** (403/429, challenge page, known blocker) are listed and do not. A refusal is not a missing page, and that matters more from a CI runner than a laptop, because datacenter IPs get walled far more aggressively.
  - Anything grading DEAD or SUSPECT is **re-checked once after a pause** before being reported, because rate limits are transient and real rot is not. Without it, hitting the same hosts twice in an hour produces a page of false alarms — SiteGround answers 202 with a meta refresh to `/.well-known/sgcaptcha/`, which is a wall, not a dead link.
  - Distinctions worth keeping: `content-type: application/pdf` with a non-PDF body is a **lying server** (the Thermo/Cytiva trap, SUSPECT); a `.pdf` URL answered with `text/html` means a **bot wall handed us a page instead of the file** (BLOCKED — `docs.nlr.gov` does this to Node while serving curl the real 2 MB PDF). And a TLS error is not a dead host: NASA and `.mil` hosts chain to roots Node does not carry but browsers and curl do, so `UNABLE_TO_VERIFY_LEAF_SIGNATURE` is reported as its own thing rather than "no response".
  - Hosts needing their API instead of their page are handled directly: PMC through E-utilities (comma-joined ids, so 137 links cost two requests), NTRS through `/api/citations/`, `openknowledge.fao.org` through its DSpace `pid/find` endpoint. Redirect targets are graded too, since many links are DOIs that land somewhere else.
  - `yt.js audit` grades the same way: oEmbed 400/401/403/404 means gone or embedding-off, but a 429, a 5xx or a socket error is **UNCHECKED**, listed and not failed. It used to treat every error as GONE, which from a CI IP would have filed an issue claiming every video died at once.
  - In the workflow, `set -o pipefail` in the check steps is load-bearing: the default shell is `bash -e` with pipefail off, so `node … | tee` would report tee's status and every month would look clean regardless.
- **Video link rot**: `node build/yt.js audit [sheet-slug]` re-checks every video already stored in the entries and reports `GONE` (deleted, private, or embedding switched off) and `RETITLED` (the stored title no longer matches the live one). Exits non-zero when anything is gone, so the monthly link-rot routine can run it alongside the further-reading check.
- **The YouTube endpoints each answer half the question.** Watch pages captcha-block curl. The innertube `search` endpoint works fine. The `player` endpoint now returns `playabilityStatus: UNPLAYABLE` for *every* anonymous caller because the streaming URLs are bot-gated, so **its status field is useless** — but the `videoDetails` it returns (duration, view count, live flag) are still correct. - **Illustration lightbox**: clicking a card illustration opens it full size. The image is wrapped in an `<a>` pointing at the image file rather than a button, so the click works without JS (it just shows the file) and the image becomes keyboard-reachable; app.js intercepts the click and shows the overlay instead. Esc, the backdrop, and the close button all dismiss it, and focus returns to the link. The lightbox sits at `z-index:70`, above the shortcuts panel, and is checked first in the Esc chain; while it is open the `/`, `?` and `j`/`k` shortcuts are swallowed, since they would act on a list the reader cannot see. - **Illustrations**: `build/generate-images.js` is sheet-driven off `sheets/<slug>/images/prompts.json`.
  ```bash
  node build/generate-images.js anchor nuclear-reactors    # the style-anchor image
  node build/generate-images.js batch nuclear-reactors 2   # 2 candidates per entry, conditioned on the anchor
  node build/generate-images.js edit <png> "<instruction>"  # cheap targeted iteration
  ```
  Output goes to a scratch dir (printed on start, override with `$IMG_OUT`), never straight into the repo — **every image gets human eyes before it is copied to `sheets/<slug>/images/NNN.jpg`**. The Gemini key is read from `../.gemini_api_key`, one level ABOVE the repo root; never commit it. All sheets share one house style so the illustrations look like a set; a sheet can override it via `"style"` in its prompts.json. Image-edit mode is much cheaper than regenerating. Prompt-writing lesson from robot-end-effectors: name the one piece of geometry that identifies the subject, and for anything gripping or holding, ask explicitly for a side view with the object clearly lifted, or the model draws it resting on a surface.
  - **The test is how much geometry the subject's defining moment carries.** Ask what a reader has to see to tell this subject from its neighbor, then ask how precisely that thing has to be drawn. A *static object* (a coil of strip, a wafer, three finished parts) or a *simple contact* (a stream falling into a cup, a drill in a hole, a stylus resting on a face, a jet on a kerf) comes back right. **Convergence** (powder streams meeting a melt pool), **immersion** (parts hanging into a bath), **enclosure** (a beam finding a seam inside a chamber), a **shaped cavity** (closed dies), **multi-part topology** (a clamp unit with tie bars), and **internal engagement** (teeth meshing, brushes on a commutator, staying coaxial) all come back wrong, and no amount of prompting fixes it.
    - Measured on a 12-entry manufacturing test with 4 candidates each: 6 of 12 usable, and the split fell exactly along that line. Passed: sand casting, drilling, rolling, waterjet, photolithography, CMM. Rejected outright: DED, electron-beam welding, electroplating, optical polishing, injection molding, closed-die forging.

Injection molding drew a vertical press, which is compression molding, entry 029.

Failing fast is safe because it skips files that already exist, so a re-run fills the gaps. Two other failure modes seen the same day, both transient and both fixed by re-running: bare `fetch failed` (6 of 21 images in one batch) and HTTP 503 under high concurrency. Do not run more than about two sheets at once.

  - **The last seven sheets were tiled on 2026-08-14** — power-electronics, recycling-and-circularity, medical-imaging-and-devices, communications-systems, construction-tech, maritime-and-shipping and in-space-systems, 49 tiles, every sheet carrying its own `style` override. Five rules came out of that run:
    - **Name the color trap sheet by sheet.** The shared no-copper list does not cover what each domain is actually colored: fiber jackets and patch cords are yellow, excavators and site gear are yellow, recycling bins are green, spacecraft are wrapped in gold foil, hulls are red below the waterline. Each of the seven overrides names its own list, and maritime also has to forbid water outright, because a waterline turns all seven of its tiles into the same picture of a ship at sea.

    - **Say how big the real thing is when the model draws a machine as a part.** Both first MRI candidates came back as a small blind-bored fitting that read as a pipe coupling or a camera. Adding "drawn as a room-sized machine rather than a small part", two plinths under it, and "the background is clearly visible through the opening" produced a scanner.

Regenerate when geometry has to come out. Note in `_partNote` when an installed tile is an edit, since the prompt alone will not reproduce it.
    - Operationally: the image API returned 503 on roughly two thirds of requests that day. Because the script skips files that already exist, the way through is many short passes with two sheets running at once, not one long run — a single pass produced 3 of 14 images in 40 minutes, and repeated passes over two sheets filled 14 of 14 in about the same time. Candidate framing also varies more than candidate subject: prefer the one that fills the frame, because a thin subject with wide margins is nearly invisible at 74px.

And it drifts toward heavy solid fills unless the style says the drawing should read as line art rather than a rendered solid model. - **Landing page**: sheets group under `category` headings from sheet.json. The running order and the one-line intro under each heading both live in **`shared/categories.json`** — Energy, Robotics and Manufacturing, Materials, Semiconductors, Computing and Photonics, Defense and Aerospace, Biotechnology — and anything not listed falls in after those in order of first appearance, with no intro. It used to be first-appearance for everything, which meant adding a sheet could silently reshuffle the page. **Add a new category to that file when you add a sheet that introduces one**; the build fails naming any listed category whose intro is missing or blank, the same way `groupBlurbs` fails. The intros are in that file rather than in build.js because they are reader-facing prose, and prose living in a `.js` file is prose `style.js` never lints — it now reads `categories.json` on a full run (skipped when a single slug is named, since the file belongs to no sheet). Cards show a glyph beside the title, then `question` (italic accent line), `blurb`, and entry count.
- **Per-sheet glyphs** live in `build/glyphs.js`, one 24×24 line drawing per slug, hand-authored for the same reason `icons.js` hand-rolls the favicons: at 22px this is pure geometry, and a raster generator cannot hold a 1.5px stroke. They are one file, not a field in each sheet.json, because the set only works if it reads as a set. `glyph(slug)` returns `''` for an unknown slug and the card simply loses its icon — no reserved gap, no shifted baseline, because the glyph sits inside the title's flex row rather than above it. **The build now fails on a sheet with no glyph** (in `composeLanding`, beside the `categories.json` intro check). That forgiving runtime behavior is correct for a sheet whose glyph has not been drawn yet and a silent regression the rest of the time: on 2026-08-14 an edit that inserted a new key into `GLYPHS` overwrote `quantum-technologies`, and 28 icons where there should be 29 is not something anyone notices by looking. The reverse check is deliberately absent — a glyph with no sheet is exactly what a sheet in progress looks like, and failing on it would block the workflow the rest of this file recommends.
  - **Distinct silhouette, not distinct detail.** Four glyphs had to be redrawn after looking at them at actual size: two touching circles for H₂ read as an infinity symbol, two rotated ellipses for an atom merged into one almond and swallowed the nucleus, a hub with three spokes for a turbine was just a letter Y, and gripper fingers ending in horizontal stubs read as a table. The semiconductor wafer was abandoned entirely — its flat is a 2.4-unit bite out of an 18-unit disc, invisible at this size, and adding a die to say "wafer" turned it into a record button. A pinned chip has a spiky outline nothing else in the set has.
  - **An inline `style` attribute outranks the stylesheet.** The sibling spans on a sheet card carry `style="display:block"`, and copying that onto the glyph's wrapper silently beat `.sh{display:flex}`, dropping the icon back inline against the title. It looked like a CSS bug and was a specificity one.
  - **`--ink3` is the metadata gray, not a prose color.** The category intro started on it and measured **2.80:1** on the light background at 13.5px, under the 4.5:1 AA needs. The card blurb is the same size and the same kind of thing to read; it uses `--ink2` and gets 5.65:1, so the intro does too. Glyphs take `--accent`, matching the `.sq` question line beside them, and carry no opacity dimming — at 2.38:1 in dark mode a 1.5px stroke has nothing left to give up.
- **About page**: `shared/about.html` is the methodology page, rendered to `site/about/index.html` and linked from the landing footer. It uses `{{N_SHEETS}}`, `{{N_ENTRIES}}`, `{{N_VIDEOS}}`, and `{{YEAR}}` placeholders, all computed at build time so the prose can't go stale as sheets are added. A global search box (shared/landing.js, inlined at build) lazily fetches every sheet's `data.json` on first focus and searches names/groups/parts/card text across all sheets, linking to `<slug>/#<anchor>`.
- **Filter-state URLs**: the explorer mirrors its state into the query string (`p` = part ids, one param per facet id with comma-joined option keys, `q` = search, `sort` = sort id, `dir=desc`) via history.replaceState, and restores it on load — filtered views are shareable links. "Reset filters" clears filters but deliberately keeps the sort.
- **Sorting**: the sort dropdown offers Catalog order, Name, and every ordered non-multi facet (auto-derived per sheet); an asc/desc toggle appears when sorting. Sorted views render flat (no group headers); entries lacking the facet always sort last. Range facets sort by their highest band.
- **How options inside one facet combine** is decided by the facet's *type*, because the data decides it — see `matchesExcept` and `narrows()` in app.js. A `multi` facet is a set of independent tags an entry can carry several of, so picking two means "carries both" and **each pick narrows** (semiconductor Device: Logic + Memory = 18 of 32). A `single` facet holds one scalar and a `range` facet holds one span, so there picking two can only mean "either", and each pick widens. **AND is not available on `single` facets and never will be**: across all 18 sheets, 408 of 408 two-chip pairs on single facets contain zero entries, because no entry has two tool costs or two supplier counts. Range facets can AND (47 of 103 pairs are non-empty) but the union is the natural read of a span. Each facet's tooltip states its own rule, generated from the type by `COMBINE_NOTE` in build.js, so no sheet has to remember to say it.
- **Facet chip counts**: every chip carries the number of entries it would leave visible, and dims at zero. The base set depends on which way the facet combines. A **narrowing** facet keeps its own selection applied (`matchesExcept(x, null)`), so each chip's number is literally "click me and you will have N left" — verified across 5,040 filter states, not just simple ones. A **widening** facet must drop its own selection (`matchesExcept(x, facet.id)`), or picking one band would read every unselected sibling as zero when each would in fact add entries back; there the number is what the band contributes, and it equals the result count only when it is the sole pick in its facet. Recomputed on each render, one pass over the entries per facet. The count sits in a `.fn` span marked `aria-hidden`, with the chip's `aria-label` rewritten to "Label, N matching" so it isn't lost to a screen reader; the plain label is stashed on the element as `_label` when the chips are built, because the chip's own markup has already been through `innerHTML`.
- **Sticky results bar** (`#resBar`): a floating "N / 212 · Reset · Top" pill that appears once `.filters` has scrolled off the top. It lives *inside* `#view-explorer`, so `.explorer{display:none}` hides it on the guide and h2h tabs with no extra state; `updBar()` still checks the active class so `aria-hidden` stays honest. Hidden with `visibility`, not `display`, so it drops out of the tab order and can still animate. Reset is hidden unless something is actually filtered (`filtered()`, which deliberately ignores sort, matching "Reset filters").
- **Keyboard shortcuts**: `/` focuses search, Esc clears it then blurs, `j`/`k` step between card headers, `?` opens the help panel. The card headers are already `<button>`s, so Tab, Enter and Space work natively — **do not add an Enter handler**, it would fire alongside the native activation and toggle twice. The arrow keys only move the cursor when a header already holds focus, so they keep scrolling the page the rest of the time. Discoverability is one `/` badge inside the search box, shown only under `(hover:hover) and (pointer:fine)` since it is noise on a phone. That badge is why `.search` is a `<div>` and not a `<label>`: a label swallows clicks on any interactive child, and a button inside one is invalid anyway. The landing page's own `.search` is still a label — it has no badge.
- **Group blurbs**: sheet.json `groupBlurbs` maps every group name to a short purpose phrase shown in the list group headers (hidden on mobile); the build fails if the map and the entries' group names don't match exactly. Class-level `blurb`s appear on the part tiles only.
- **Prerendered cards**: `shared/render.js` holds the only copy of the entry-card markup. build.js `require`s it to fill `{{LIST}}` in page.html, and injects the same file into the page ahead of app.js, so the HTML that ships and anything a filter redraws come from one implementation — a second copy in build.js would drift and show up as a flash of different content on load. It is a factory (`makeRenderer(SHEET, EMBED_OK)`) because both callers need those two ambient values, and the build passes the same `clientSheet` object the page gets. **Verify a change to it by diffing shipped against redrawn**: set a detached div's innerHTML to `R.listHTML(P, true)` and compare with `#list.innerHTML`, so both go through the same parser — comparing the raw string against `innerHTML` reports false differences, since the browser rewrites `<path/>` to `<path></path>`.
  - The client is told whether it got prerendered markup via `PRERENDERED`, and skips the first `innerHTML` write when the initial state matches what shipped (catalog order, nothing filtered). A URL carrying filters or a sort re-renders normally. **The artifact flavor is deliberately not prerendered** — it always runs JS, nothing crawls it, and the entries are already in the page as JSON, so prerendering would double the file for no reader who benefits. That is why the flag exists: a page built without prerendering must render on load or the list stays empty.
  - Cost: the entries ship twice, once as HTML and once as the `P` JSON the client needs for search and filtering, which is +50–90% on the wire after gzip (manufacturing 190K → 357K). First paint is still earlier, because the browser draws streaming HTML instead of waiting on JS. The duplication is removable — filter by hiding prerendered cards instead of rebuilding them, and take the search haystack from each card's `textContent` — but that is a rewrite of render/filter/sort, not a tweak.
  - `<noscript>` in page.html opens every card body and hides the controls that cannot work, so a reader without JS gets all 750 entries as a plain document.
- **Placeholder substitution goes through function replacers.** `String.prototype.replace` rewrites `$$`, `$&`, `` $` `` and `$'` in the replacement even when the search pattern is a plain string, so `"Costs $$"` in a guide or an entry would silently corrupt the page (`$&` expands to the placeholder itself, `` $` `` to everything before it). Every `.replace('{{X}}', …)` in composeBody passes `() => value` instead, which is exempt. Nothing in the repo trips it today; `{{LIST}}` alone injects ~900KB of authored prose, so keep new injections in the same form.
- **Head tags and social unfurls**: `head()` in build.js emits the whole document head for all three page kinds (sheet, landing, about) — title, meta description, canonical, OpenGraph, Twitter card, and the icon links. It takes `path` (the page's location under `SITE`, `''` for the landing page) and `up` (the prefix back to the site root, `../` for sheet and about pages), because Pages serves everything from `/reference/` and a root-absolute `/favicon.ico` would 404. Sheet descriptions come from `sheet.blurb`; the landing description appends the entry, sheet, and category counts at build time so it cannot go stale. Attribute values go through `escAttr`, not `esc` — `esc` leaves quotes alone, which is fine for text nodes and not for `content="…"`. The artifact flavor has no head at all (the Artifact tool supplies one), so none of this touches it.
- **No `og:image`.** A card worth having would print the sheet's title, that needs font rasterization, and nothing in this dependency-free repo can do it. One generic banner on all 18 sheets unfurls worse than a clean title-and-description card. If it ever gets added, the images have to be real PNG/JPEG — every major unfurler ignores SVG for `og:image`.
- **JSON-LD and the sitemap** (added 2026-08-13). `head()` takes an optional `ld` graph and emits it as `application/ld+json`; the artifact flavor has no head at all, so it is excluded by construction. Sheet pages carry `WebSite` + `CollectionPage` + `BreadcrumbList` + an `ItemList` of every entry + a `DefinedTermSet` of every glossary term; the landing page carries an `ItemList` of the sheets; the about page a plain `WebPage`. The point is the **anchors** — all 952 entries are addressable at `#slug`, and nothing in the markup said those ids were a list of named things.
  - Entry URLs come from `makeRenderer({}, false).slug`, not a second copy of the same one-liner, because a URL that doesn't match the id the card actually carries is worse than no URL. `makeRenderer` needs no sheet for this; `slug` closes over nothing. A build-time check worth repeating after any change to either: parse each page's JSON-LD and confirm every advertised fragment matches a `<div class="card" id="…">` on that page (952 of 952 at the time of writing).
  - **Glossary terms are named, not defined.** The definitions are already in the page as visible text in the glossary tab, so repeating them in JSON-LD would roughly double the largest text block on the page to tell a crawler what it can already read. 1,047 terms, names only. Whole cost is 4.7 KB gzipped on the biggest sheet against a 396 KB page.
  - **`JSON.stringify` does not escape `<`**, so the emitter rewrites every one to `\u003c`. One entry name or definition containing `</script` would otherwise close the block early and spill JSON into the document. The escape is invisible to consumers, because `\u003c` and a literal `<` are the same character to any JSON parser.
  - **The sitemap lists 26 pages, not 952 anchors.** A fragment is the same URL to every crawler, so listing the anchors would submit 24 URLs 952 times. No `<lastmod>`: the honest value is when the content last changed, and file mtimes are checkout time in CI, so every URL would claim it changed on every deploy — an absent lastmod is ignored, a wrong one trains crawlers to ignore the real ones.
  - **It is not auto-discovered.** `robots.txt` is read only from a domain root, and this repo serves `/reference/`, not `humbaventures.com/`. That root robots.txt exists and allows everything but carries no `Sitemap:` line, and it is served by something outside this repo. Either add `Sitemap: https://humbaventures.com/reference/sitemap.xml` there or submit the sitemap in Search Console; writing `site/robots.txt` would accomplish nothing.
- **Analytics: GoatCounter** (added 2026-08-13). The site code is the `GOATCOUNTER` constant in build.js — public by definition, since it ships in the page source, so it is not a secret. Empty disables analytics everywhere, which is what a fork wants: nobody else's traffic should land in this account. No cookies and no cross-site identifier, so no consent banner. The script is `async` and emitted last in `head()`; the artifact flavor has no head, so it cannot pick it up.
  - **Pageviews alone would answer almost nothing here.** Every entry on a sheet shares one URL, so a 212-entry sheet is a single number. `gcEvent()` in app.js adds two event kinds: `reference/entry/<sheet>/<card-id>` when a card is opened, and `reference/tab/<sheet>/<tab>` when a tab is clicked. The sheet slug comes from the last path segment rather than a new field in `SHEET`, which would have grown every sheet's shipped JSON just to name an event.
  - **Events are prefixed with the mount path so they do not commingle** with the main humbaventures.com site in a shared dashboard. `GC_PREFIX` is injected alongside `EMBED_OK`/`PRERENDERED` and is *derived from `SITE`*, not written out again, so moving the site cannot leave the events pointing at the old mount point; it is `''` for a root-mounted site, which is the correct prefix there. Pageviews need no equivalent — `count.js` prefers the canonical link over `location`, and every page sets one, so a pageview already records as `/reference/<slug>/`.
  - **Counted once per page load**, via a `Set`. A reader who opens the same card five times read it once, and counting five would overstate the exact number the events exist to measure.
  - **`expandAll` is deliberately not instrumented.** One click opens every card, and 212 events would drown the ones a reader actually chose.
  - **Tab events fire only when `setHash` is true**, which is the click path. Restoring a tab from the URL on load passes false, so a shared link does not register as the recipient choosing that tab.
  - `count.js` declines to fire on localhost, private IPs, `file:`, inside a frame, and on prerender, so local previews never count. To keep your own visits out of the numbers on the live site, run `localStorage.setItem('skipgc', 't')` once in the browser console on humbaventures.com.
  - Verify a change to any of this by stubbing the client rather than sending real hits: set `window.goatcounter = {count: o => sent.push(o.path)}`, then click a card twice, a tab, and Expand all, and check the captured paths.
- **Favicons are generated, not committed**: `build/icons.js` writes `favicon.svg`, `favicon.ico` (16/32/48) and `apple-touch-icon.png` (180) into `site/`, which is gitignored, so CI regenerates them on every deploy. It hand-rolls a PNG encoder over Node's built-in `zlib` and packs the ICO directory itself rather than taking a dependency; shapes are pure geometry (a rounded tile plus three bars) so no font is involved. `inRound()` is shared by the rasterizer and the SVG writer so the two flavors can't drift. The `<link>` for the .ico deliberately declares only `sizes="32x32"` even though it holds three: an unqualified .ico outranks the SVG in Chrome, and the SVG is the one that stays sharp on a high-DPI tab.
- **The Biotechnology category** (2026-08-13). Four sheets were added alongside `bioprocessing` and the category was renamed from Bioprocessing, since only one of the five is about manufacturing. The set is deliberately layered rather than parallel: `therapeutic-modalities` asks what a therapy should be, `bioprocessing` asks how it gets made, and `gene-editing-and-delivery`, `sequencing-and-synthesis` and `diagnostics-and-assays` are the tools. That mirrors the pair the hardware side already had, where `engineering-materials` asks what a part is made of and `manufacturing-processes` asks how.
  - **Overlap between sheets is by question, not by noun.** AAV, lentivirus and LNP appear on both `therapeutic-modalities` and `gene-editing-and-delivery`, and that is correct: the first asks whether a one-time durable therapy is the right modality and what it costs, the second asks what the carrier holds, where it goes and how long the editor persists. When adding to either, check which question an entry answers rather than whether the word already appears elsewhere.
  - **`sequencing-and-synthesis` states specs as bands on purpose.** Instrument specifications in that field move faster than anything else in this repo — read lengths, accuracies and prices have changed by large factors across single product generations — so point figures would be wrong within a year. The lede says so explicitly. If that sheet is ever refreshed, re-band rather than re-quote.
  - Facets that have to serve mixed entry types are workable if the tip says how to read both: `cargo` on the editing sheet means machinery size for an editor and capacity for a carrier, and matching those two numbers is the sheet's central decision. A facet whose meaning changes silently between entries is not.
- **Seven sheets added on 2026-08-14**, taking the repo from 28 to 35: `power-electronics` (Energy), `recycling-and-circularity` (Materials), `medical-imaging-and-devices` (Biotechnology), `communications-systems` (Computing and Photonics), `construction-tech` and `maritime-and-shipping` (a new **Infrastructure and Transport** category), and `in-space-systems` (Defense and Aerospace). 262 entries, all seven with guide, head-to-head and glossary.
  - **Three of them layer under an existing sheet rather than duplicating it**, the way `gene-editing-and-delivery` layers under `therapeutic-modalities`. `communications-systems` part III is spectrum filings, service economics and how an operator buys capacity, while `space-launch-and-satellites` keeps the spacecraft and the terminal. `in-space-systems` sits one level below that sheet's part VII: where it has one entry on servicing, this has RPO sensors, docking mechanisms, cryogenic transfer and thermal budgets. `power-electronics` treats as a converter design problem what `grid-and-transmission` treats as a transmission choice. Every agent writing those parts was required to read the neighboring sheet's entries first, and each entry names where the other question lives. **Check that boundary before adding to any of the four.**
  - **The taxonomy was written before the entries, not discovered from them.** Each sheet got a table of every entry with its exact frontmatter, and the agents wrote to it. A script diffing all 262 files against those tables found **zero** unintended deviations — every difference was a correction made deliberately during review. That is the mechanism that keeps a sheet coherent when eight agents write it in parallel, and it is cheap: the tables live in `SEVEN-SHEETS-PLAN/` outside the repo.
  - **Facet values are the thing reviewers must actually check.** Prose came back good almost everywhere; the facet tags were where the errors were, and nearly all of them were mine rather than the writers'. Mammography tagged Class II when tomosynthesis is Class III and that is the bar a new entrant faces; CT tagged above $3M when most CT is not; orthopedic robots tagged above $3M against prose saying $0.8–1.5M; neuromodulation and cochlear implants a band above an ICD sitting beside them; storable propellant transfer tagged as demonstrated when Progress has done it operationally since 1978. A wrong facet is worse than wrong prose because it silently removes an entry from the filter a reader is using.
  - **Two facet options shipped with zero entries and were removed**, since a permanently dim chip is clutter: a Research maturity band on recycling and a Class I band on medical imaging. Run a facet-coverage count before calling a sheet done.
- **CI runs Node 24 everywhere, on node24 actions** (2026-08-13). All three workflows pin `node-version: 24` — the LTS line, supported until 2028-04-30 — and every action is pinned to a major that declares the `node24` runtime (`checkout@v7`, `setup-node@v7`, `configure-pages@v6`, `upload-pages-artifact@v5`, `deploy-pages@v5`). This fixed two things that had gone unnoticed since the repo was created: the deploy ran Node 20, which was already end-of-life on the day it was committed and carried no comment saying why that number; and it ran a *different* Node than style.yml and linkcheck.yml, so a version-sensitive bug would have shipped to production without any check catching it. Pin the version as a number, not `lts/*`, so a new LTS cannot silently change what the deploy builds on.
  - **`upload-pages-artifact` has to go to v5 even though GitHub's own starter workflow still shows v3.** v3 wraps `upload-artifact@v4`, which is node20, and that transitive pin is where the last deprecation warning came from — it names an action the workflow never mentions, which is why it looks unfixable.
  - Checked before the bump, and worth repeating if the version moves again: Node 24 and Node 25 produce **byte-identical** HTML and sitemap across all 26 pages. Only `favicon.ico` and `apple-touch-icon.png` differ, and inflating their IDAT chunks gives identical raw pixels, so that is deflate packing (Node 24 bundles zlib 1.3.2.1, Homebrew's Node 25 links 1.2.12) and not a behavior change. Hash the whole `site/` tree under both, then diff pixels rather than bytes on anything that comes back different.
